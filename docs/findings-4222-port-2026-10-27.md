# Build 42.22 pre-release: port, stock profile, optimized vs stock (2026-10-27/28)

Game: 42.22 pre-release, revision `e013dbb529` (Steam install, native Linux). Port: worktree `~/pzopt-wt/port4222`,
branch `port-42.22` from origin/master `adef1841` (uncommitted). Desktop: 5120x2160 @ 165 Hz, RTX 4090, power profile
`performance` (~5.3 GHz). All runs through the queue, `--launcher direct`, an empty `-Dpzopt.userOptionsFile` (defaults),
`--no-dashboard`, the converted 42.22 bench template. Run dirs: `harness/runs/p4222-*` in the worktree.

## 1. The port

- 57 of the 139 override classes changed bytes in 42.22, 54 changed code; 11 had merge conflicts, all resolved by hand.
  Method and every change: `docs/override-edits.md`, "Port to Build 42.22".
- API changes we had to follow: `PZArrayList` gone (`getObjects()` is a `List`, our TimSort scratch replaced by
  `List.sort`), `Core.tileScale` a constant 2 (1x packs removed), the `GameMode` enum (Last Stand removed,
  `Core.getInstance().isTutorial()`), `getSkyLightLevel(int playerIndex)`, tile definitions moved to
  `media/tile_definitions/` (our boot preload read the old path and fell back to stock until fixed), `isSoundListener()`
  (a listener now ticks its emitter every frame: our idle skip respects it), `savedWorldVersion` 249 → 251.
- Checks: `build.sh` audit 9,229 unedited methods, 0 mismatches; for the 82 methods we edit that 42.22 also changed, 72
  carry exactly 42.22's change in our build and the other 10 were read by hand (type-only, intended edits, relocated
  bodies); `scripts/test.sh` all pass (`ForwardDirectionScratchTest` accepts the jar's new `new Vector2()`).
- Not in the installed build: local master's unpushed commits (incl. `pzopt.ModLuaProfile`, 589f2f3f). The port is
  origin/master only.

## 6. Optimized Louisville ~8 % slower uncapped than capped (cause found in §8)

| Optimized Louisville | runs | mean | p99 | 1 %-low | game thread busy |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| capped (VSync 165, MangoHud, JFR) | 153, 148, 145 | 149 | ~14 ms | ~70 | 90-92 % |
| uncapped (VSync / VRR off, no MangoHud, no JFR) | 137, 132, 144 | 138 | 17-19 ms | ~53 | 96 % |

Not run-to-run spread (the sets do not overlap) and not the extra tools (the capped runs carry more of them). The game
thread is the wall in both; uncapped every phase is ~10 % longer a frame (update ~3.9 → 4.6 ms, render in step, same
shares), so it looks machine-wide rather than one phase. GPU clock equal; sysmon has no CPU clock. Unverified guess:
uncapped, the next frame starts while the previous frame's worker-side work (bone math, CharDraw, tile record,
batches) still runs, so they contend for cores and caches. Next: a JFR pair uncapped vs `frameRate=244` uncapped-off
(same tools on both), worker-thread CPU per frame from `pzopt-threads.out`, and the CPU clock per run.

## 8. Cause of the capped > uncapped gap: capped runs hold back ~350 zombies (2026-10-28)

The capped runs are not faster at the same work. They simulate and draw fewer zombies. `zombieSpawnSpread` queues the
zombies the native population turns real away from the player (> 25 squares). At a frame cap,
`zombieSpawnSlackBudgetUs` sizes each frame's spawn budget as what the last step left of the cap interval
(`ZombiePopulationManager.pzoptDrainSpawns`). Louisville never reaches 240 fps (steps ~8 ms against a 4.17 ms interval),
so that budget is always 0. Only entries older than `zombieSpawnMaxAgeMs` (3 s) are created, at most
`zombieSpawnAgedCapUs` (600 us) of them a frame, and the queue never empties. Uncapped, `Pacing.capIntervalNs()` is 0,
the slack rule does not apply, and the normal budget empties the queue within `zombieSpawnDrainFrames`.

- **The 12 original 42.22 runs** (console `zombie spawn spread:` lines, route window): all 6 capped runs had 100-750
  zombies waiting, and 2,500-2,900 were created only once aged. All 6 uncapped runs had 0 waiting and 0 aged.
- **Same on 42.21** (local master 22f546a7 + dev keys, worktree `~/pzopt-wt/capalt`, `devFrameSections`; tools matched:
  no MangoHud, no JFR):

  | Louisville | zombies updated / frame | fps | spawn queue in the route |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | capped (VSync + 240), slack budget on (default) | 2176-2207 | 111, 116, 134 | 277-682 waiting |
  | capped, `zombieSpawnSlackBudgetUs=0` | 2497-2502 | 114, 109 | drains to 0 |
  | uncapped | 2491-2586 | 90, 119, 109 | 0 |

**Ruled out on the way:**

- **Within-run A/Bs on one scene** (`devGtAlternateKeys=zombNoop,frameCapAlt[,vsyncAlt]`, a dev key that switches
  `FrameCap.uncappedNow` / `Display.setVSyncEnabled` every 1 s, ABBA):
  - cap alone: +0.21 / -0.11 ms a frame;
  - cap + VSync: -0.54 / +0.24 ms.

  No effect, because both halves share one population.
- **JFR within the run** (`jcmd JFR.start/stop`): +0.78 / +0.05 ms (no speedup).
- **CPU idle states within the run** (`/dev/cpu_dma_latency`):
  - 300 us: +4.8 %;
  - 10 us: +2 %, both noise;
  - 0 us: -18 %, because idle siblings busy-poll, which shows what SMT contention costs the game thread.
- **Clocks and scheduling** (`/proc` sampler): ~5.2 GHz, run-queue wait ~1 % in every arm.
- **GC:** 3-5 ZGC cycles in every route.

That every game-thread phase was ~10 % longer uncapped (§6) is the larger population, not a slower machine.

**Consequences:**

1. Capped Louisville numbers (here and in earlier passes) measure a smaller horde than uncapped ones whenever the frame
   rate stays below the cap. Compare like with like, or check the `zombie spawn spread:` lines.
2. In play, at a cap the game cannot reach, zombies at the edge of the loaded area keep arriving seconds late, with a
   standing backlog of hundreds. That is more than the "timing only" the key was adopted as. A possible fix: when the
   slack is 0 for many frames in a row, fall back to the normal budget (`zombieSpawnBudgetUs` / backlog over
   `zombieSpawnDrainFrames`). It needs the maintainer's decision.

## 9. The other discrepancies (2026-10-29, game on 42.22 again)

### 9.1 Stock 42.22 Louisville 22 fps vs 38-44 on 42.21: run settings, not 42.22

The 42.21 runs at 38-44 fps (2026-10-14/15 in the DB) were launched with `--gc g1 --option tieredZombieUpdates=true`.
The 42.22 runs in §3 used neither: the game's launcher JSON (ZGC) and `tieredZombieUpdates` off in options.ini.
Same build, same day, stock 42.22, Louisville, uncapped at `frameRate=244`:

| Stock 42.22 Louisville | runs | mean |
|---|---|---|
| ZGC (launcher JSON), tiered off | 22.5, 25.0 | 23.8 |
| `--gc g1`, tiered off | 25.5, 27.2 | 26.4 |
| `--gc g1 --option tieredZombieUpdates=true` | 43.5, 40.9 | 42.2 |

That is 42.21's 38-44 fps. 42.22 did not regress. ZGC costs stock ~10 % here; 2026-10-24 showed the same (G1 31.9 vs
ZGC 24.7). The 42.21 runs at 24-32 fps (10-13 / 10-23) were the runs without these flags.

### 9.2 Master port "5-11 % slower than origin on every route" (§7): mostly session drift

§7 compared origin runs from 10-27 with master runs from 10-28. Back to back, alternating the two installs (`--install
opt` from each worktree, uncapped, `devFrameSections`):

| Route | origin | master | master vs origin |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rosewood spin | 401, 390, 411 | 371, 399 | -4 %, sets overlap |
| 120 km/h drive | 641, 641 | 658, 664 | +3 % |
| Rosewood 120 drive | 577, 587 | 571, 578 | -1 % |

On Louisville the spread between runs is larger than the gap. Frame time depends on the horde's size (+0.48 ms a frame
per 100 zombies updated), so the table fits frame time against zombies updated a frame and the build. 22 runs, three
batches, runs `om-lou-*`, `tg-*`, `t3-*`, `t4-*`:

| Louisville, vs origin at the same zombie count | ms a frame | translucent pass |
|---|---|---|
| master, defaults (10 runs) | +0.30 ± 0.33 | 1.07 ms (origin 0.86, 6 runs) |
| master, `translucentGridsPerFrame=false` (4 runs) | -0.12 | 0.85 ms |
| master, `modLuaProfile=false` (2 runs) | not separable from the noise | 1.00 ms |

Run-to-run spread is 0.55 ms a frame (sd, ~7 %). The one cost that stands out of the noise is master's
`translucentGridsPerFrame` (default on since 2026-10-25, the picnic-table seam fix): the translucent tile pass is
+0.2 ms a frame in the horde (+25 %). That is the visual fix's price, not a regression. The ~10 % gap of the first
Louisville ABBA (origin 127 vs master 114 fps) did not repeat.

### 9.3 Harness runs measure ZGC, players get G1

Harness runs keep the launcher JSON's ZGC unless `run.sh --gc g1` is passed. Players get G1 from `pzopt.GcChoice`
(`gcMode=g1`). Master port, Louisville uncapped, ABBA (runs `t5-*`):

| GC | fps | ms a frame at 2,650 zombies | p99 | p99.9 | collections in the run |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ZGC (harness default) | 124.2, 133.0 | 8.39, 7.60 | 18.0-18.5 ms | 24-28 ms | 20-21 concurrent cycles, pauses ~0 ms |
| G1 (`--gc g1`, what players get) | 129.2, 126.1 | 7.90, 8.05 | 17.5-17.6 ms | 41-48 ms | 44-45 young pauses, 420-448 ms in all (~10 ms each) |

The mean frame rate is the same. The tail is not: G1's young pauses (~10 ms each, every ~1.5 s in the horde) double
p99.9 in the optimized build. Harness tail numbers on Louisville are therefore better than what a player on the
default `gcMode=g1` sees. Stock differs: on G1 its mean is ~10 % higher (§9.1). Whether `gcMode` should stay G1 for players, or harness runs should measure G1, is
the maintainer's decision.

### 9.4 Summary of the causes

| Discrepancy | Cause |
|---|---|
| Optimized Louisville capped > uncapped (§6) | the capped runs hold back 300-700 zombies (§8, spawn slack budget 0 below the cap) |
| Stock 42.22 Louisville 22 fps vs 42.21 38-44 | run flags: the 42.21 runs had `--gc g1` + `tieredZombieUpdates=true` (§9.1); no 42.22 regression |
| Master port 5-11 % slower than origin (§7) | session drift across days; back to back within ±4 % on the GPU routes; on Louisville only `translucentGridsPerFrame` (+0.2 ms a frame) stands out of a 7 % run-to-run spread (§9.2) |
| Harness vs player tails | harness on ZGC, players on G1: same mean, p99.9 24-28 vs 41-48 ms on Louisville (§9.3) |
